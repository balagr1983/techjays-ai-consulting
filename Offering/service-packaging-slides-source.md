# Slide Source Document: The AI and Automation Opportunity Study

Purpose of this document: source material for generating a presentation deck. It
defines a 7-slide deck. Each "SLIDE" section below specifies the slide title, the exact
on-slide text, and a layout suggestion. Use the on-slide text as written; do not
paraphrase, shorten, or add new claims. Slide notes are guidance for the generator and
the presenter, not on-slide content.

## Design directives (apply to the whole deck)

- Font: Google Sans (or closest available sans-serif). Large, legible type; minimal
  small print.
- Colors: dark navy (#0B2545) for titles and structure; teal (#1AC0C6) as the primary
  accent; coral (#F26B5B) as the secondary accent for emphasis and numbering.
- Style: clean, minimal, one idea per slide, generous white space. No decorative stock
  imagery. Slides 1 and 7 are dark navy backgrounds with white text; all others are
  light.
- Tone: professional, plain language, no exclamation marks.

## SLIDE 2 of 7: The service

Section label: The service

Headline: A six to eight week study that turns "we know AI matters" into a costed,
prioritized roadmap.

Supporting line (below headline): Fixed fee. We map how the business actually runs,
identify where process re-engineering, automation, and AI can grow revenue per
employee, and deliver a stack-ranked plan that can be executed immediately, in-house
or with us.

Three numbered points (the pitch), displayed large:

1. Most companies are stuck between AI pilots and real impact. The stall is
   organizational, not technological.
2. We study the business the way engineers do: process by process, quantifying where
   the leverage is.
3. Leadership gets a prioritized plan with numbers they can defend to their board, in
   six to eight weeks.

Footnote (small text at the bottom, keep on this slide):
MIT NANDA, The GenAI Divide 2025: about 95% of enterprise gen AI pilots show little to
no measurable P&L impact, attributed to workflow integration, not model quality.
McKinsey, The State of AI 2025: workflow redesign is the strongest of 25 factors
driving AI's bottom-line impact; only 21% of companies have done it.

Layout: headline top, three numbered statements as the body (numbers in coral),
footnote in small muted text.

## SLIDE 3 of 7: The method

Section label: How it works

Headline: Re-engineer first. Automate next. AI where it changes the economics.

Supporting line: Every process goes through the same three layers, in order, and every
opportunity comes out costed, plotted on return vs effort, and stack-ranked.

Four chips or boxes in a row:

- Layer 1, Re-engineer: simplify the process before any technology is added.
- Layer 2, Automate: rules, integrations, and workflow automation where certainty is
  enough.
- Layer 3, Apply AI: including agents, where judgment, language, and 24/7 operation
  genuinely pay.
- Output, Prioritize: every opportunity costed and stack-ranked on return vs effort.

Two columns below:

What it is:
- A quantified operating plan
- A scored assessment and an opportunity portfolio on a return vs effort matrix
- A sequenced roadmap with a concrete first 90 days

What it is not:
- A strategy deck
- A technology audit
- A staffing pitch

Layout: four chips across the middle, two comparison columns below (the "what it is"
column accented teal, the "what it is not" column muted gray).

## SLIDE 4 of 7: What the seller walks in with

Section label: Selling it

Headline: What the seller walks in with.

Three cards side by side:

Card 1, The offer card: six to eight weeks, fixed fee. Deliverables by name: readiness
scorecard, opportunity portfolio on the return vs effort matrix, initiative outlines
with build / buy / partner options, first-90-days plan, executive readout.

Card 2, The sample report: an assessment report built from real Techjays engagements;
actual process maps, target designs, and roadmaps with client identifiers removed.
Answers the "show me what I get" buyer. Next collateral investment.

Card 3, The qualification profile: mid-market, roughly 100 to 500 employees.
Operations- or GTM-heavy. Board or CEO pressure on AI. Pilots that have not moved the
P&L.

Layout: three equal cards, each with a small colored top border (teal, coral, navy).

## SLIDE 5 of 7: Openers and objections

Section label: Selling it

Headline: Three questions that open the door. Four answers that keep it open.

Three door-opener questions (numbered, displayed prominently):

1. What did your last AI pilot change in the P&L?
2. If your team doubled tomorrow at the same cost, where would you point it?
3. Who in your company can draw how a customer order actually flows, end to end?

Objection and answer table (two columns):

| The objection | Our answer |
|---|---|
| "Why not a big firm?" | Same study, a fraction of the fee, and a plan you can build rather than a strategy deck. |
| "You will just tell us to hire you." | Fixed fee independent of any build decision. Every recommendation carries build / buy / partner options. The client owns everything. |
| "Our data is not ready." | Data readiness is one of the things the study assesses. It is not an entry ticket. |
| "We can do this in-house." | Individual productivity, yes. Department-level re-architecture needs dedicated structure from outside the daily grind. |

Layout: questions as a numbered list on top (numbers in teal), objection table below.
If one slide is too dense, split into two slides: questions, then objections.

## SLIDE 6 of 7: Why Techjays

Section label: Why Techjays

Headline: Three reasons, each one verifiable.

Three cards side by side:

Card 1, We build what we recommend: estimates come from people who design and deliver
these systems every week, so the plan is buildable, not theoretical. Because we can be
asked to execute it, we live with our own advice: an incentive no pure-strategy firm
has.

Card 2, Engineering depth at a mid-market price: a comparable study from the big firms
runs several times our fee and stops at strategy altitude. We work at workflow level:
the actual steps, handoffs, and systems, where the value hides.

Card 3, Independence by design: fixed fee, no obligation to build with us, build / buy
/ partner options on every recommendation, and the client owns every artifact. If the
right answer is their in-house team, the report says so.

Bottom line (single sentence under the cards): Proof today: delivery heritage across
AI and data programs, including a three-week logistics process redesigned to run in
three to four days. The first pilot's case study slots in here.

Layout: three numbered cards (01, 02, 03), proof sentence as a footer strip.
