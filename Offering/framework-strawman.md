# Techjays Assessment Framework - Strawman v0.1

> Draft for the Philip working session, 21 Jul 2026. Purpose: give the call something
> concrete to react to. Everything here is proposed, nothing is locked.

## Why we need a named framework

Every credible player scores clients against a named, multi-dimension framework (PwC: 12
domains, 0-100 score; Accenture + CMU: 8 dimensions; Thoughtworks: FOREST, 6 dimensions;
McKinsey Rewired: 6 dimensions). The framework is the product's spine: it makes the
assessment repeatable, comparable, and defensible, and it is what the market expects to
see on page one of a proposal.

## Name candidates (pick one, or use these to spark a better one)

1. **Techjays AI Value Compass** - the compass metaphor matches what we sell: direction
   plus a stack-ranked route. Output artifact naturally becomes "your AI Compass Report".
2. **BLUEPRINT** - positions the deliverable as a build-ready plan, which is exactly our
   skin-in-the-game differentiation: others hand you a study, we hand you a blueprint we
   can build from.
3. **Techjays AI GPS (Grounded Priority Scan)** - leans into the stack-ranking: where you
   are, where to go, the fastest route, recalculated as things change (echoes PwC's
   "scores update as initiatives progress" retention mechanic).

Recommendation: option 1 or 2. Option names should survive being said aloud to a CEO.

## Proposed dimensions (six, workflow-anchored)

The research core (common across all published frameworks) plus our differentiator:

| # | Dimension | What we score | Why it earns its place |
|---|-----------|---------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Strategy & Leadership | AI ambition tied to revenue, margin, client experience; CEO-level governance ownership | The three-value tie from Philip's pitch; CEO governance oversight is the factor most correlated with bottom-line impact (McKinsey) |
| 2 | Workflows & Operating Model | Process-level mapping of how work actually flows; redesign readiness | Our differentiator. Workflow redesign is the single highest-leverage factor for AI EBIT impact, and only ~21% of adopters have done it. This is the DND muscle. |
| 3 | Data | Availability, quality, access, integration of the data the priority use cases need | Universal dimension; scored per use case, not in the abstract |
| 4 | Technology & Engineering | Current stack, integration surfaces, build-vs-buy posture, technical debt that blocks AI | The engineering-rigor claim needs an engineering dimension |
| 5 | Talent & Adoption | Skills, capacity, change readiness, KPI/measurement discipline | Biggest adoption barrier per Deloitte; fewer than 1 in 5 track gen AI KPIs |
| 6 | Governance, Risk & Trust | Responsible AI, security, compliance, agent governance | Increasingly expected; only 1 in 5 have mature agent governance |

Scoring: 1 to 5 per dimension with defined level descriptions (publishable maturity
bands), rolled up to a headline readiness score. Keep the mechanics simple; the
benchmarking compendium (a PwC-style asset) grows engagement by engagement.

## The signature output: the 2x2

Every identified opportunity is scored on **ROI (value)** vs **cost/effort (feasibility)**
and plotted on the Techjays 2x2. Quadrants: Quick Wins (high ROI, low cost - do first),
Big Bets (high ROI, high cost - plan properly), Fill-ins (low ROI, low cost - bundle),
Avoid for now (low ROI, high cost). Everything stack-ranked into a costed, sequenced
roadmap. This chart is the one slide the CEO keeps.

## The bias-objection answer (write it into the method)

Because Techjays also builds, the method must visibly protect the advice:
- The study is fixed-fee and priced independently of any build decision.
- Every recommendation carries a build / buy / partner / do-nothing option, with at
  least one non-Techjays path where one credibly exists.
- The client owns all deliverables outright; no lock-in artifacts.
