Working draft v1 · internal review

A structured approach to the AI and automation opportunity study

How we would map, quantify, and sequence the opportunities across sales, business development, marketing, and customer service: process re-engineering first, then automation, then AI, ending in a roadmap the leadership team can execute.
1. The objective

What Waites leadership wants to achieve

Where this starts

The CEO sees AI as a transformational shift rather than an incremental productivity tool, and wants every function, not only software development, to understand it and get the most out of it.

The working goal

Leadership has been chartered to build a plan by end of 2026, with the approach finalized by end of September. The guiding question: roughly double revenue per employee over the next two to three years, by growing revenue with the current team rather than reducing it.

The situation today

Real AI initiative exists in pockets, but it is not coordinated and there is no roadmap. Department heads are being asked to reimagine their functions, which is difficult to do from inside the function alone.

Our starting position

Technology is unlikely to be the limiting factor. What is missing is a clear, quantified picture of how work flows today and how it could flow instead. Producing that picture is the purpose of this engagement.

The questions this study answers

  • Which processes, re-architected, move revenue per employee the most?
  • Where do AI and automation genuinely outperform simpler fixes, and where do they not?
  • What is each opportunity worth, and what would capturing it take?
  • In what order should Waites act, starting with the first 90 days?
  • Which data connections (NetSuite, HubSpot, internal systems) are needed along the way?

2. Approach

Three layers, applied in order

1

Process re-engineering

First, simplify: remove steps, handoffs, and waits before any technology is added. Some of the cheapest gains require no technology at all.

2

Deterministic automation

Then automate what is rule-based: integrations, triggers, and workflow automation between NetSuite, HubSpot, and internal systems, where certainty is enough.

3

AI

Only then apply AI, including agents, where judgment, language, and continuous operation genuinely change the economics of the process.

Scope

In: Sales, Business Development, Marketing, Customer Service, plus the data connectivity that underpins them (NetSuite, HubSpot, internal systems).
Out: Software development, which already has its own roadmap.

Evaluation lens

Every opportunity is assessed on revenue capacity: does it help the same team produce more qualified pipeline, more closed revenue, and better retention? Cost effects are recorded where they appear.

3. Method

The same study cycle, department by department

1. Understand

Structured interviews with leads and the people doing the work, supported by pre-shared questionnaires and observation.

›

2. Map

As-is workflows: inputs, outputs, handoffs, systems touched, volumes, cycle times, and where the hours go.

›

3. Re-architect

Target-state design through the three layers, including where humans stay in the loop.

›

4. Quantify

Projected gain per opportunity, with the assumptions stated and testable.

›

5. Prioritize

All opportunities plotted on return vs effort and stack-ranked into a sequenced roadmap.

Worked example: the business development function

A team of eight to ten people produces a target number of qualified meetings per month. The study maps how a meeting is actually made: list building, research, outreach, follow-ups, scheduling. Each step is then classified: which parts a cleaner process removes, which are deterministic automation, and where agents working continuously and in multiple languages change the meeting economics. The output is a costed target design with a projected meetings-per-person multiple, comparable against the other opportunities on the same matrix.

Return →

Quick wins

High return, low effort. First in the sequence.

Big bets

High return, high effort. Planned and sequenced deliberately.

Fill-ins

Low return, low effort. Bundled with adjacent work.

Not now

Low return, high effort. Documented and revisited later.

Effort and cost →

Every opportunity is plotted, stack-ranked, and costed on this matrix.

4. Engagement model and team

Hybrid: onsite and remote, by design

Onsite for the critical work

Re-architecture working sessions, observation of the actual work, executive workshops, and the final readout are conducted in person, where they are most productive.

Remote where it is efficient

Suitable discovery work is handled over video calls, pre-shared questionnaires, and document review, completed before travel. The onsite duration flexes with the pre-work: the more discovery completes remotely, the shorter the onsite window needs to be, which also reduces cost.

Working across time zones

With the engagement lead onsite in the US and the solution designer offsite in India, material gathered during the US working day is developed further overnight and is ready for review the next morning.

6. Deliverables

What the engagement produces

The roadmap report

1Executive summary
2Current-state process maps for business development, sales, marketing, and customer service
3Opportunity portfolio on the return vs effort matrix, stack-ranked
4Initiative outlines for the top opportunities: target workflow, layer classification, projected gain, indicative effort, and build / buy / partner options for each
5Data connectivity assessment and sequencing
6Sequenced roadmap with a concrete first 90 days

Executive readout

A working session with the executive team to review the portfolio, agree the sequence, and identify owners, in addition to the written report.

Independent recommendations

Every initiative carries build, buy, and partner options, including paths that do not involve Techjays. Where the in-house team is the right answer, the report says so. Waites owns all artifacts produced during the engagement.

Grounded estimates

Effort and feasibility estimates come from a team that designs and builds these systems in delivery work, so the roadmap reflects implementation reality.

Appendix

Pre-work sample: Business Development questionnaire, excerpt

1Walk us through a lead's journey from first touch to a booked discovery call: each step, every system touched, every handoff between people.
2What is the monthly meeting target per person, what is typical attainment, and where do the hours go in a normal week?
3Which outreach steps are templated or sequenced today, and which are crafted individually each time? What tooling is in place?
4What information is consulted before reaching out (HubSpot fields, NetSuite history, external research), and what is missing?
5What are the main reasons a booked meeting does not become a qualified opportunity?
6If the team's capacity doubled tomorrow at the same cost, where would the extra effort go first, and why?

Full questionnaires for all four departments follow once the scope is confirmed. Each is designed to be answerable in under an hour, and completed pre-work directly shortens the onsite window.
