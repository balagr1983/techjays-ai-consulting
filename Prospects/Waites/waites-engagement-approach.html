Reimagining Waites for the Agentic AI Era

A structured approach to finding, sizing, and sequencing the opportunities that double revenue per employee, across sales, business development, marketing, and customer service.
Prepared by Balasubramanian GR (Bala), Techjays  |  For discussion with Gerry Perkel  |  Jul 2026
1. The challenge

The goal is clear. The highest-leverage places to act are not, yet.

The ambition

Double revenue per white-collar employee within two years, growing the business with the team you have, not by cutting it. A coordinated roadmap by end of 2026, with the approach settled by end of September.

The reality today

Strong functional teams and real pockets of AI initiative, but no coordinated map of where agentic AI creates the most leverage across the commercial and support functions. Department heads are being asked a question that is not reasonable to answer alone: "reimagine your function".

Our starting belief

Technology is not the limiting factor. The limit is a clear, quantified picture of how work flows today and how it could flow instead. That picture is what this engagement produces.

What this engagement answers

  • Which processes, re-architected, move revenue per employee the most?
  • Where does agentic AI genuinely beat simpler fixes, and where does it not?
  • What is each opportunity worth, and what would it take to capture it?
  • In what order should Waites act, starting with the first 90 days?
  • What data foundations need connecting along the way?

2. Our approach

Re-engineer before you automate. Automate before you add AI.

1

Process re-engineering

First, simplify. Remove steps, handoffs, and waits that exist only because the process grew up around people and legacy systems. Often the cheapest gains live here, with no technology at all.

2

Deterministic layer

Then automate what is rule-based: integrations, workflow automation, triggers between NetSuite, HubSpot, and internal systems. Where certainty beats probability, deterministic wins.

3

Agentic AI layer

Only then deploy agents, where judgment, language, research, and 24/7 scale genuinely change the economics: outreach, qualification, multi-language coverage, always-on responsiveness.

Scope

In: Sales, Business Development, Marketing, Customer Service, plus the data connectivity that underpins them (NetSuite, HubSpot, internal systems).
Out: Software development, which already has its own roadmap.

The lens is revenue, not headcount

Every opportunity is assessed the same way: does it help the same team produce more meetings, more qualified pipeline, more closed revenue, better retention? Cost savings are recorded where they appear, but capacity multiplication is the goal.

3. How the study works

The same disciplined cycle, department by department.

1. Understand

Structured interviews with leads and doers, pre-shared questionnaires, observation of the real work, not the org-chart version of it.

›

2. Map

As-is workflow maps: inputs, outputs, handoffs, systems touched, volumes, cycle times, and where the hours actually go.

›

3. Re-architect

Target-state design through the three layers: what disappears, what becomes deterministic, where agents take over, where humans stay in the loop.

›

4. Quantify

Projected gain per opportunity: capacity freed, revenue lift, cost avoided, with the assumptions stated and testable.

›

5. Prioritize

Every opportunity plotted on return vs effort, stack-ranked into a sequenced roadmap Waites can start executing immediately.

Example: the BDR function

Eight to ten people produce a target number of qualified meetings per month. We map how a meeting actually gets made: list building, research, outreach sequences, follow-ups, scheduling. Then we ask, step by step: which parts vanish with a cleaner process, which are deterministic automation, and where would agents working around the clock and in any language change the meeting economics entirely? The output is not "use AI in BD"; it is a costed target design with a projected meetings-per-person multiple.

4. Engagement model and team

Hybrid by design: onsite where it matters, remote where it is efficient.

Onsite for the critical work

Whiteboard re-architecture sessions, shadowing the real work, executive workshops, and the final readout are done in person, where they are dramatically more productive.

Remote where it is efficient

Suitable discovery work moves to video calls, pre-shared questionnaires, and document review before travel. The more that lands before we fly, the shorter and sharper the onsite window becomes, and the lower the overall cost. The onsite duration flexes with how much we can complete this way.

Follow-the-sun advantage

With the engagement lead onsite in the US and a solution designer offsite in India, work continues around the clock: what is whiteboarded during your business day returns as worked-up target designs and estimates by the next morning.

Concrete artifacts, owned by Waites, actionable without us.

A working session with the CEO and executive team, not a deck-drop: the portfolio debated, the sequence agreed, owners identified.

Straight advice, no lock-in

Every initiative carries build, buy, and partner options, including paths that do not involve Techjays. Waites owns every artifact outright. If the best answer for a given initiative is your in-house team, the report will say so.

Estimates you can trust

Effort and feasibility numbers come from people who build these systems every day, not from a slideware library.
