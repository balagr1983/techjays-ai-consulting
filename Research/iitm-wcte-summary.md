# IIT Madras - Walmart Center for Tech Excellence (WCTE) - Brochure Summary

> Source: `walmart brochure 2026.pdf` (received 17 Jul 2026 from Bala's friend at the
> CoE). Center of Excellence in Manufacturing Analytics, IIT Madras, headed by
> Prof. Raghunathan Rengaswamy, established under an MoU with Walmart Global Tech.
> Segment: Indian manufacturing MSMEs. Not our client segment, but several patterns are
> directly instructive for the Techjays practice.

## What they are

An industry-academia CoE focused on "digital empowerment" of manufacturing MSMEs:
affordable, easily deployable AI/ML and digitization solutions plus skill development.
Team: industry experts, IITM faculty, PhD students, post-docs, project associates.
Themes: profitability, safety, sustainability, quality, operational efficiency.

## What they offer (three lines)

### 1. Productized software platforms (not bespoke consulting)
- **DATAPY** - cloud data visualization/analytics; raw data (even Google Sheets) to
  interactive dashboards; production bottleneck detection.
- **FABRIC** - a "mini ERP" for MSMEs: PO-centric order tracking, batch planning,
  inventory, RFQ, quotations.
- **MANGO** - flow-based drag-and-drop AI/ML workflow studio with pre-built templates;
  cloud execution on docker.
- **Tools:** Predictive Maintenance (rotary assets/bearing faults via sensors),
  Non-Conformance Redressal system (digitizes legacy quality records; knowledge graph +
  LLM guided root-cause analysis), OCR (invoices/POs to searchable data), KAATRU
  (hyperlocal air-quality monitoring and reports).

### 2. Skilling and training
Training sessions on Industry 4.0, IoT, AI/ML, digital manufacturing for MSMEs (and for
faculty/students). Topics: predictive maintenance, process optimization, data analytics,
digital transformation strategy.

### 3. Ecosystem/channel partnership - FaMe TN
MoU with FaMe TN (Facilitating MSMEs of Tamil Nadu): CoE provides the software package,
supports deployment on FaMe TN's cloud, MSMEs access tools **at subsidized rates**
through FaMe TN, with implementation support and hands-on training from the CoE.

## Business model read

This is **adoption enablement, not fee-based consulting**: Walmart (CSR/tech-ecosystem
funding) + academic institution + government-adjacent channel (FaMe TN) deliver
subsidized products and training. The "consulting" is embedded in tool implementation
and training, not sold as a study. Social proof: named testimonials from MSME operators
(Amma Alloys Coimbatore, Shreyas Machine Tools Bengaluru, Accutech Laser, etc.).

## What Techjays should take from it

1. **Third independent confirmation of the advisor-who-builds-assets pattern.** McKinsey
   has QuantumBlack Labs, West Monroe has Intellio, WCTE has DATAPY/FABRIC/MANGO.
   Reusable platform assets lower delivery cost and make the offering tangible. A
   lightweight Techjays assessment toolkit (data collectors, scoring app, dashboard
   templates) would follow the same logic.
2. **Third instance of someone-else-subsidizes-the-client:** Microsoft partner funding
   (Slalom), AWS Marketplace private offers, and now FaMe TN subsidized rates. Worth
   asking in every deal: is there a vendor, government, or ecosystem program that can
   co-fund the client's pre-study?
3. **Skilling as a packaged offering line.** Deloitte's 2026 survey says insufficient
   worker skills is the single biggest AI adoption barrier; WCTE shows training packaged
   alongside tools. An upskilling module inside or after the Techjays pre-study
   (train the client team on the recommended stack) is a natural margin line and answers
   the top adoption barrier.
4. **Credibility co-branding, again:** Walmart + IIT Madras mirrors Accenture + CMU SEI.
   If Techjays wants a credibility anchor for the practice, an academic or ecosystem
   partnership is a proven move at both the top and bottom of the market.
5. **Named-client testimonials** carry the brochure. The pilot-phase case study and
   testimonial in our roadmap (Phase 3) is the equivalent asset; collect quotes early.
6. **Not a competitor** for Techjays' target segment (US mid-market/enterprise); relevant
   as low-end subsidized competition only if an India MSME motion ever matters.
