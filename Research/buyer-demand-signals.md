## The core stall: adoption is high, value is not

- **McKinsey State of AI (Nov 2025):** 88% of organizations use AI in at least one
  function, but nearly two-thirds have not begun scaling AI across the enterprise. Only
  39% attribute any enterprise-level EBIT impact to AI, and most of those put it under 5%.
- **McKinsey State of AI (Mar 2025):** only 1% of executives in developed markets call
  their gen AI rollout "mature"; more than 80% of gen-AI-using organizations see no
  tangible enterprise-level EBIT impact.
- **MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" (via Fortune, Aug 2025):** roughly 95% of enterprise gen AI
  pilots deliver little to no measurable P&L impact. Attributed not to model quality but
  to a "learning gap" with organizational workflows.
- **IBM CEO Study (May 2025; 2,000 CEOs):** only 25% of AI initiatives delivered expected
  ROI; only 16% scaled enterprise-wide.

**Sales translation:** the addressable market for a pre-study is nearly everyone: most
companies are stuck between piloting and scaling, and the stall is organizational
(workflows, skills, governance), which is what a readiness study diagnoses.

## Direct evidence of pre-study demand

- **IBM CEO Study:** 64% of CEOs admit they invest in some technologies before
  understanding their value, driven by fear of falling behind. That is a paid
  value-assessment waiting to happen.
- **Deloitte State of AI 2026 (3,235 leaders, 24 countries):** 42% believe their strategy
  is highly prepared for AI, but fewer feel prepared on infrastructure, data, risk, and
  talent, exactly the dimensions a readiness assessment scores. 74% aspire to grow
  revenue through AI vs 20% actually doing so.
- **MIT SMR + BCG (Nov 2025):** organizations are deploying agentic AI before they have
  a strategy for it (35% adoption, 44% planning). Adoption cycles are compressing
  (traditional AI: 8 years to ~72% adoption; gen AI: 3 years to ~70%), so the
  look-before-you-leap window is shrinking, which argues for short, fast studies.
- **GAO (Apr 2026):** all four reviewed federal agencies said they had no playbook for AI
  procurement; DoD's Project Maven found early contracts lacked AI-specific requirements
  needed to hold vendors accountable. Skipping pre-implementation requirements definition
  creates concrete downstream failures, even at the most sophisticated buyers.

## Skin in the game: evidence on the builder-advisor question

- **MIT NANDA (via Fortune):** AI tools bought from specialized external vendors/partners
  succeed about 67% of the time; internal builds succeed about one-third as often. The
  strongest third-party datapoint FOR the "we advise and build" model.
- **Deloitte 2026:** insufficient worker skills is the single biggest barrier to
  integrating AI into workflows; only 1 in 5 companies has a mature governance model for
  autonomous agents. Buyers lack the capability to act on advice alone, which favors an
  advisor who can execute.
- **IBM CEO Study:** top innovation barriers are lack of expertise/knowledge, silos, and
  risk aversion; 50% of CEOs say rapid tech investment left them with disconnected,
  piecemeal technology.
- Direct survey evidence on the bias-perception risk (advisor who builds recommends
  building) was NOT found in this pass. Peer interview question 8 targets exactly this
  gap; it may end up being our proprietary insight.

## Budget misallocation (an opener for the pitch)

- **MIT NANDA:** more than half of gen AI budgets go to sales and marketing tools, while
  the strongest ROI was found in back-office automation. A stack-ranked opportunity study
  literally corrects a documented misallocation.

## Fee-model pressure at the top of the market (context)

- ~25% of McKinsey's global fees are now outcome-based (McKinsey UK managing partner,
  Nov 2025). BCG expects AI work to be ~40% of revenue by 2026; Bain ~30% heading to 50%.
  The big firms are racing to AI-ify their own model, and outcome-based pricing may
  trickle down as a client expectation; worth deciding early whether Techjays would ever
  price the pre-study against realized savings.
