# BCG: The CEO-Board AI Knowledge Gap ("Split Decisions" survey + CEO's Guide)

> Added 5 Aug 2026 from Bala. Two related BCG pieces: (1) "Split Decisions: The BCG
> CEOs and Boards Survey" (published 4 May 2026; 625 leaders: 351 CEOs, 274 board
> members, companies with USD 100 million+ revenue, global, private and public sectors);
> (2) "The CEO's Guide to Closing the AI-Knowledge Gap with Your Board" (article,
> 4 Aug 2026; experts Julie Bedard and Kristy Ellmer). The guide PDF is saved in this
> folder and parsed in full.

## The core finding

Surface alignment, fundamental disconnect: CEOs and boards appear aligned on AI
governance and value creation, but 75% of board members rate their AI understanding as
on par with or better than peers, while CEOs say many directors cannot distinguish AI
hype from reality, calibrate value-creation timelines, or grasp how AI reshapes growth.
Left unaddressed: stalled decisions, weak investment choices, and pilots that never
reach enterprise-scale value.

## The numbers

From the guide (Aug 2026): **61%** of CEOs believe boards are rushing AI
transformations; **35%** believe boards overestimate AI's ability to replace expertise
rather than augment it; **37%** say boards lack an informed view of how AI is reshaping
growth strategy.

From the survey press release (May 2026): 50%+ of CEOs say boards need a better grasp
of hype vs reality; CEOs put **35%** of their own performance evaluation on achieving
AI ROI while boards put it at **27%** (the accountability perception gap); ~80% of both
groups support AI-literacy requirements for incoming board members.

*(Figure note: the press release reports 33% "overestimate replaceable human
capabilities" and 40% "lack informed growth views"; the later guide prints 35% and 37%.
Cite whichever document you name; the guide is the more recent primary.)*

## BCG's five moves for CEOs (from the guide)

1. **Personally envision and articulate the AI strategy** - what AI means for value
   creation, competitive advantage, operating model, leadership, risk. Ellmer: "Where
   are we just keeping up, and where is AI actually creating competitive advantage?
   Otherwise, it can look like you're implementing AI just for the sake of doing it."
2. **Lead the learning yourself** - the CEO personally runs board upskilling sessions
   with live tools, rather than delegating to a CTO or external experts (Bedard).
3. **Plan immersive learning beyond board meetings** - visits to AI-first companies,
   customer discussions, competitor scans, exposure to live AI use cases.
4. **Articulate augmentation vs replacement** - boards push replacement harder than
   CEOs; talk about AI "in a much more differentiated way" showing where it substitutes
   for human work and where it complements it (Bedard).
5. **Build an AI-savvy transformation committee** - 3 or 4 directors with deeper
   expertise who "question things a little more", possibly only for the transition
   years (Ellmer).

Closing point: CEOs carry more of the AI decision burden than either side thinks they
should; responsibility must be shared across the senior leadership team.
