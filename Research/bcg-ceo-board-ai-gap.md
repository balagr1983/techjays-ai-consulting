## Methodology

Global survey of 625 leaders: 351 CEOs and 274 board members, companies with at least
USD 100 million annual revenue, private and public sectors.

## The findings

| Stat | Figure |
|------|--------|
| CEOs who say their boards are RUSHING AI transformation | 61% |
| Board members who rate their AI knowledge as on par with or ahead of peers | 75% |
| CEOs who say boards lack informed views on AI's impact on growth | 40% |
| CEOs who say boards overestimate the human capabilities AI can replace | 33% |
| CEOs who say boards need a better grasp of AI hype vs reality | 50%+ |
| Share of CEO performance evaluation tied to achieving AI ROI (per CEOs) | 35% |
| Same, per board members (the perception gap) | 27% |
| Both groups supporting AI-literacy requirements for new board members | ~80% |

BCG's recommendations (Julie Bedard, Judith Wallenstein): CEOs should personally lead
board AI upskilling rather than delegating to a CTO or outside consultant; teach the
substitute-vs-complement distinction; build genuine understanding, not surface
awareness. The Next Web adds a structural point: when the CEO is the board's only AI
teacher, the board cannot independently evaluate the CEO's AI strategy.

## Why this matters for the Techjays practice

1. **It validates our sharpest pitch line with the biggest brand in the room.** Our
   deck promises "a prioritized plan with numbers they can defend to their board."
   BCG now quantifies exactly that pressure: 61% of CEOs feel boards rushing them, 50%+
   say hype distorts boardroom judgment, and 35% of their own evaluation rides on AI
   ROI. The buyer of our study is a CEO squeezed between a hyped board and delivery
   reality; the study is the defensible answer they hand upward.
2. **A citation upgrade for the sales material.** Alongside MIT NANDA and McKinsey,
   this adds board-level urgency from BCG. Candidate line: "61% of CEOs say their
   boards are rushing AI transformation (BCG, May 2026). A costed, prioritized roadmap
   is how a CEO answers a rushing board." Deck-safe with attribution.
3. **A door-opener for the CEO-coach channel.** Gerry's clients ARE these CEOs.
   Candidate question for the toolkit: "How much of your own evaluation this year is
   tied to AI ROI, and what will you show the board for it?" Worth sharing the piece
   with Gerry as a value-add touch, since it speaks his language.
4. **A possible service attachment.** BCG says CEOs should personally lead board
   upskilling. Our executive readout can be packaged to serve double duty: the CEO
   walks into the board with our portfolio and roadmap as the teaching material. A
   board-session variant of the readout is a natural, low-cost add-on to the study.
5. **Perception-gap sales fact:** CEOs think 35% of their evaluation rides on AI;
   boards think 27%. Even the accountability is misaligned, which is precisely a
   governance/alignment gap an external, quantified study helps close.
